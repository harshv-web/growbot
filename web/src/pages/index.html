Born

It wakes up in a tablet from your drawer.

An old Fire 7, rooted, becomes its first body. Six senses switch on one by one: eyes, ears, touch, light, balance, voice. Then it sleeps, and dreams, and writes the first line of who it is. Nobody else can write that line.

Learns

It learns to walk. Nobody codes the steps.

Give it two legs and it plays: wiggles, stands, leans, falls, gets up. A tiny model inside it learns how its own body tips, and a rhythm tunes itself stride by stride until it can walk to its bed and charge.

Feels

It has moods you can see, and read.

Praise makes it glow. Hours alone make it lonely. A fall scares it; learning a step makes it proud. Its feelings are numbers you can open, and they change how it walks, what it remembers and when it talks to you.

Play with its feelings
Travels

Tap your keys. It moves into your pocket.

Its home is the tablet, but it lives wherever you are: the round screen on your keychain, the e-paper on the back of your case, your iPhone on its own little legs, even your scooter rides. One soul, one memory, always in sync. Only one body moves at a time.

Remembers

It knows your life, and keeps it to itself.

Your mornings, your metro, your groceries, the things you promised people. It remembers them privately, forgets what you ask it to, and shows its work. It speaks English and Hindi, and it plugs into the apps you already use.

Shares

It meets other creatures, and trades what it learned.

Walking styles, reflexes, the sound of a pressure cooker: skills move between souls as small packs with no personal data inside. It even understands GrowBot souls.

Jeevo grows from GrowBot, Brit Cruise's phone-brained robot that learns everything in its own life: a constitution only you can edit, a dream that is the only thing allowed to rewrite who it is, and movement that emerges instead of being scripted. Jeevo carries that soul across many bodies. Personal, noncommercial, built in public in Bengaluru.
